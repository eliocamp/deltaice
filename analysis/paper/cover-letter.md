In this manuscript, I estimate the response of sea ice concentration to temperature as a joint function of near-surface air temperature and sea ice concentration itself. 
This relationship explains about 50% of the observed variability in monthly sea ice concentration tendency and provides a physically interpretable framework for separating thermodynamic from dynamic variability. 
I show that, surprisingly, sea ice melts at high concentrations at very low temperatures. 
I apply this framework to CMIP6 models and show that errors in the estimated relationship are strongly correlated with errors in the simulated Antarctic sea ice seasonal cycle, suggesting that deficiencies in the models' simulated sea ice come from the representation of ice response to air temperature forcing rather than from errors in the atmospheric forcing.
